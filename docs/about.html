About this project

A machine learning pipeline that predicts English Premier League results from historical data, and an honest account of how well it does.

No model beat the bookmaker. On the holdout seasons the best model scored a log loss of 0.9959. The bookmaker's closing odds scored 0.9893, and lower is better. Model accuracy is about 50 to 52 percent.

This is a statistics project, not betting advice.

What it does

The pipeline downloads match results going back to 2000/01 from football-data.co.uk and builds features for each match using only information available before kick-off: recent form, shots, rest days and Elo ratings. One version of the features also includes the bookmaker's odds.

It then trains several models and compares them:

  • Classifiers that give home win, draw and away win probabilities directly: logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost and LightGBM, with calibrated and ensemble versions.
  • Goals models (a Poisson GLM and XGBoost) that predict each side's expected goals. A Dixon-Coles scoreline grid turns those into result probabilities, the most likely scoreline, and the chances of over 2.5 goals and both teams scoring.
  • Baselines: always pick the home team, the historical class frequencies, and the bookmaker's closing odds.

Each model is trained on three feature sets: A uses form only, B adds Elo ratings, and C adds the market odds. The site only displays the files the pipeline writes. Nothing is computed in your browser beyond formatting.

How validation works

Football results are a time series, so every split is in date order and nothing is shuffled. A model is only ever scored on matches played after the ones it was trained on.

  • Development seasons. Models are chosen by walk-forward validation: train on every season up to a point, score the next season, then move forward one season and repeat.
  • Holdout. The 2024/25 and 2025/26 seasons, 760 matches, are kept aside and used once for the final numbers.
  • Live. Completed matches of the current season are reported separately. That sample is too small to rank models.

Automated checks in the pipeline confirm that no feature uses information from the match it describes or from later matches.

Limitations

  • No model beat the bookmaker. Best model holdout log loss is 0.9959 against 0.9893 for closing odds. Closing odds already reflect team news and the money of well-informed bettors, which this pipeline never sees.
  • Accuracy is about 50 to 52 percent on the holdout seasons.
  • Draws are almost never predicted. About a quarter of matches are drawn, but a draw is rarely the single most likely outcome, so the models and the bookmaker almost never pick one.
  • No injury or lineup data is used. Suspensions, transfers, managerial changes and cup or European fixtures are also invisible to the models.
  • The best model leans on the market. With odds as an input, a model mostly learns to copy the bookmaker. Feature sets A and B show what form and Elo do on their own.
  • Promoted teams are uncertain. They have no recent top-flight history, so their early-season numbers are borrowed from the previous season's bottom three.
  • Small differences are noise. The holdout is two seasons. Gaps of a few thousandths in log loss between models do not mean one is better.
